## The venue

**Paris Convention Centre, Pavilion 7, 3rd floor** — 1 Place de la Porte de Versailles, 75015 Paris.
The Jamboree runs 13–16 November and the whole competition is in Pavilion 7.3: fifteen judging
rooms, nine stages, fifteen villages and a few hundred booths on one floor.

Walk in at the main street entrance on the corner of **boulevard Victor and boulevard Lefebvre**.
Pavilion 7 is about **450 m further on, roughly 8 minutes**, slightly uphill, with moving walkways.
Allow fifteen minutes from the hotel door to the hall floor, not twelve.

## Getting there

- **Nearest stop:** Porte de Versailles – Parc des Expositions, **tram T3a**.
- **From CDG:** RER B towards Saint-Rémy-lès-Chevreuse to Cité Universitaire, then T3a towards Pont du Garigliano.
- **From Orly:** metro line 14 to Maison Blanche, walk 6 minutes to Porte d'Italie, then T3a.
- **Tickets:** a **Navigo Easy** card is €2 at any airport or station; load single tickets at €2.55
  or ten for €25.50. The weekly **Navigo Semaine** is €32.40 and runs Monday to Sunday — which
  for a trip landing on Wednesday 11 November means it covers only part of the stay. Ten singles is
  the simpler buy.

**Where we stay:** Courtyard by Marriott, 5 Rue Ernest Renan, Issy-les-Moulineaux — a 12-minute
walk to Pavilion 7, which is the single best logistical fact of this trip. You can go back to the
hotel between sessions, and on Monday morning you should.

## The booth

Booth **H-17**, Biomanufacturing Village, **iGEM shift B**. We share one side of a triangle with a
team on shift A, so another team is immediately beside us using the same side at different hours.

Per the team site, our hours are **Friday 14:00–18:00** and **Saturday 09:00–18:00**. The iGEM API
says shift B is Friday 13:00–17:00 and Saturday 08:00–17:00. **Resolve this on Thursday** — see
[the open questions](schedule.html#open).

Every booth gets the same kit: a standard team sign naming both teams and the village, a **55-inch
wall screen (picture only, no audio; HDMI and USB-C, cables provided)**, one high-top table
75 × 60 cm, three stools, and three **Type C/E, 230 V** outlets.

**The one rule: nothing goes on the wall.** No posters, decorations, signs, stickers or banners
attached to the booth wall. The screen is the only display, and during our hours we can use it
however we like.

### What an instructor does at the booth

Not run it. The students do, and three adults standing at a high-top table is how a booth stops
being approached. The schedule puts **one instructor on call** per booth block — in the hall, phone
on, close enough to arrive in two minutes — and leaves the other two free for sessions. The rota is
on [the four days](schedule.html); the short version is Gabriel on Friday afternoon and Saturday
morning, Chars on Saturday afternoon, and Timmy opening the booth with group 1 on Saturday at 08:30.
The full rota, with who walks past when, is on the [duty roster](duties.html#booth).

## Photographs

Posters and booths are fine, and teams are usually pleased to be asked. **Ask before photographing
people, and ask before photographing anything that may be unpublished** — some teams are sitting on
a manuscript. "May I photograph this?" also opens the conversation that gets you the mechanism,
which is what you came for.

For each team shoot three frames: the poster wide, the one figure that matters, and something with
the team name legible. Then the photos label themselves later.

## Practicalities

- **Time:** Paris is **7 hours behind Taipei** in November. Message home before 16:00 Paris time if
  you want anyone awake.
- **Weather:** about 5–12 °C, often grey with drizzle, sunset around 17:10.
- **Power:** Type C/E, 230 V, 50 Hz.
- **Money:** euro; cards taken almost everywhere.
- **App:** the official **My iGEM App** holds the badge, the live schedule and — unlike the public
  API — the speaker names and the per-team expo groups. Install it before you fly.

## When something goes wrong

**112** — the European emergency number, free from any phone, and it works without a local SIM.
**15** medical (SAMU) · **17** police · **18** fire.

**Call an instructor first.** The four instructor numbers go live with the team eSIMs; they are on
the [students' emergency page](https://timmy97-tw.github.io/releaf-jamboree/) once active, and that
page is the single source — do not maintain a second copy here.

**Help from Taiwan:** Taipei Representative Office in France, emergencies only, **+33 6 80 07 49 94**.
There is also a free worldwide emergency line for R.O.C. nationals abroad.

Stay with the group. Students do not travel alone, and an instructor knows where every student is
at the end of each day.

The whole system for this — squads, numbers 1 to 30, the three evening checks, location sharing,
the emergency cards and what to do when — is on the [duty roster](duties.html#q1).
