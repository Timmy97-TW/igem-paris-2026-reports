The team flies on **10 November** and the Jamboree opens on **13 November**. Three of those days
are ours to use, and the two hours of preparation that matter most are not packing.

This page is the instructors' version. The students' own trip page — flights, rooms, booth shifts,
materials, emergency numbers — is on the [ReLEAF to Paris site](https://timmy97-tw.github.io/releaf-jamboree/),
and the numbers below are taken from it rather than restated independently.

## Two things to settle on Thursday, not Friday

Both of these are disagreements between sources, not unknowns, and both are fixable in ten minutes
at the venue with a badge in hand.

1. **Our booth hours.** The iGEM API's expo day plan puts shift B at Friday 13:00–17:00 and Saturday
   08:00–17:00. The team site says Friday 14:00–18:00 and Saturday 09:00–18:00 — an hour out on all
   four edges. Whichever is wrong, the students are working to the team site, so fix that one.
2. **Where our stage talk is.** The API says the **Fabrication plaza stage**; the team site says the
   **Main Stage**. The API is the live source and plaza stages are where ten-minute team talks
   happen, so the plaza is far more likely. The two are not near each other, and Sunday 14:40 is not
   the moment to discover which.

Also on Thursday: **opt in to Operation Outbreak** in the app. It runs all Saturday in the
background and Sunday's debrief session is worth nothing without your own data in it.

## What to read before the plane

Not much, and none of it is long.

- **[The brief](brief.html)** — the four themes the report answers, and the working rules. Fifteen minutes.
- **[Growth tracks](tracks.html)** — the eight capabilities, and for each one the specific gap we are
  trying to close. This is what turns a session into a note worth keeping. Twenty minutes.
- **[Your own day](schedule.html)** — filter to your name. Read the `why` lines, because on the day
  you will not have time to reconstruct why a session was chosen over the one next to it.

What not to do: read the triage of all 452 teams. It exists for the booth you walk past, not for
the plane. Team talks are fill-in this year, behind a toggle on the schedule page.

## Questions, written down before you leave

Every priority session on the schedule carries one question. They are there because the difference
between a session you attended and a session you got something from is usually one question asked
out loud, and nobody invents a good one while a panel is finishing.

Read yours on the flight. If one of them is already answered on the team's wiki, replace it with a
better one and change it on this site.

## What to carry

The students' materials list covers the hardware. For an instructor:

- **A notebook that is not a phone.** Three days of plaza talks at ten minutes each do not survive
  being typed with one thumb.
- **The question list**, printed. See above.
- **A charger and an adapter.** Type C/E, 230 V. Taiwan is 110 V — check that anything you bring is
  dual-voltage, which almost everything is and chargers always are.
- **Business cards**, if the [GEMS set](https://github.com/Timmy97-TW/igem-paris-2026-reports) made
  it to print. Follow-ups agreed at the afterparty need somewhere to land.
- **A warm jacket and an umbrella.** Paris in November runs 5–12 °C, often grey, sunset around 17:10.

## The one rule that makes the reports work

**Write the notes the same evening.** Budget forty-five minutes a night, before dinner rather than
after. Three people who each write up the same day produce an evidence base; three people who write
on the flight home produce three sets of half-remembered impressions that cannot be reconciled.

The note template is in [notes](notes/), and it has the same fields every time on purpose: free-form
notes from three people never merge.
